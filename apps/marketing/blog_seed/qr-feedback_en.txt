Most unhappy customers never complain — they simply do not come back. A QR code on the table, receipt or counter gives them a ten-second way to tell you what went wrong while they are still there, and gives happy customers a way to say so too.

## Where to place a feedback QR code
- On tables, menus and receipts in cafés and restaurants.
- At the reception desk and in rooms in hotels.
- On packaging and delivery bags for online orders.
- At checkout counters, fitting rooms and service desks.

## Keep the survey short
Ask one main question and at most two or three follow-ups. A good start is the Net Promoter Score question: "How likely are you to recommend us to a friend or colleague?" on a 0–10 scale, followed by "What could we do better?". Short surveys get many more answers.

## Know where every answer comes from
Give each location its own QR code — one per branch, floor or table group. Then you can compare places and see exactly where a problem started.

## Act on the answers
- Read new responses every day and assign negative ones to a colleague.
- Add internal notes and mark a response as resolved when it is handled.
- Watch the trend of your scores week by week, not single answers.

## Respect privacy
Ask for contact details only when you really plan to reply, and only with the customer's consent. Qrumy never stores respondents' IP addresses and saves contact details only after consent.
